How it works
Reading the Atlas
!
Why this isn't a project Gantt
In project work you control the schedule. In tax resolution the IRS does. So instead of fixed start dates and durations, every case walks a stage lifecycle with uncertain dwell times. Demand becomes a probability distribution, and the real risk isn't average overload — it's deadline clustering when several IRS responses land at once.
1 Define your service lines
On Case Types & Stages, build each service line (OIC, Installment Agreement, Penalty Abatement, Audit Representation, Unfiled Returns/CNC…). For each stage set firm hours, actor (you vs. IRS), and min / expected / max months. The min/max spread on IRS stages is what powers the surge band — wider spread = more uncertainty.
2 Set billing per case type
Each case type bills as a flat fee (collected over N months from engagement) or a monthly retainer (charged every month the case is open). Flat-fee revenue is fixed at engagement, so the longer the IRS takes, the more your effective realization erodes — the Financial Outlook tracks this.
3 Load your team and caseload
On Team, set FTE, billable %, specializations, and how many cases each pro can carry. On Active Caseload, record each open case's current stage and how long it's been there. Add expected new work on Intake Pipeline.
4 Read the verdict
The dashboard computes three utilizations every month — case-load (cases vs. pro capacity), surge (P90 hours vs. capacity), and hours (expected hours vs. capacity) — and the verdict reflects whichever is binding. Use the Auto / Caseload / Surge / Hours toggle to inspect each lens.
  • TAKE MORE CASES — all constraints below the lower bound; you have slack to sell.
  • HOLD — comfortably loaded.
  • ADD CAPACITY / SLOW INTAKE — a constraint is above the upper bound.
5 Stress-test the IRS
On Scenarios, model "IRS processing slows 30%," "intake doubles after tax season," or "add an EA in March," then overlay them on the dashboard. This is how you plan around the timeline you don't control.
